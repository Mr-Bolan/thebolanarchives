## two small models

One page adjusts a base hourly amount by summed percentages, annualizes optional weekly,
monthly, or yearly overhead, prorates that overhead to the entered contract duration,
and divides the combined requirement by contract hours. A final percentage multiplies
the requirement before the page updates its result and comparison chart.

The interface calls that last percentage a profit margin. The code applies a markup
multiplier to cost; it does not solve for a target share of final revenue. The distinction
belongs beside the input, not hidden behind the chart.

The other page divides an annual rate into 26 equal periods, computes a payoff count,
and constructs a repayment table plus a separate horizon projection. It rejects payments
that do not exceed the first period's interest. The payoff expression has no separate
zero-rate branch: substituting zero produces a zero divided by zero in the logarithmic
calculation.

## what this establishes

These are observations about the reviewed JavaScript, not validated pricing or lending
models. No current rates, financial recommendations, or claims about actual repayments
are being made.

The next useful check is a set of synthetic fixtures: zero rate, missing values,
non-finite input, a final partial payment, and equivalent overhead entered at different
frequencies. None was executed in this review. A neat table cannot substitute for that
calculation contract.
