# coverage cells are not complete proof

## what the grid actually checks

The inspected modeller represents rules as ranges across confidence, severity, and count. A grid cell is classified by which rule boxes intersect it. No matches produce a hole or fallback warning; multiple matches produce overlap. A single match then passes through destination-permission and review checks.

That ordering makes ambiguity visible before selecting a destination.

But intersection is weaker than complete coverage. A narrow rule touching one corner can make a whole cell look covered. Two disjoint rules touching different parts of the same coarse cell can also produce an overlap label without sharing an exact observation.

## point checks remain necessary

The separate scenario evaluator tests an exact point against the same ranges. It reports unmatched and ambiguous observations and compares a unique result with an expected destination.

The grid is a screening surface, not a proof of exhaustive or conflict-free routing. Boundary-point fixtures and finer partitioning would help distinguish actual holes from coarse-bin warnings.
